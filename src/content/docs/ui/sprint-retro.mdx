The Retro tab of a sprint is where the team records what went well and badly, and decides what to change. Open a sprint and choose **Retro** after **Backlog**, **Taskboard** and **Capacity**. Any project member can open the tab, add points, theme them and decide them. For the points across all sprints, see [Retrospectives](/ui/retrospectives/).

## The Retro tab

A retrospective is a set of points. Each point has a category and some text, and it can have a theme. The categories are **Went well**, **Went badly**, **Can be improved** and **Not in the plan**. ShipLean shows each point as a note. People write points here. Agents write theirs with `log_retrospective` when they stop work on a story, and the conductor compiles a Feature retro when the last story closes. Both appear on the tab.

The summary at the top shows four figures. **Finished items with a retro** counts the items with a tick in Coverage against the items in Coverage that are not waiting. The total leaves out Features that are still waiting. **Recurring themes** links to Insights. **Points to decide** counts the points still waiting for a decision. **Action items raised** counts the Action items raised from this sprint's points.

Below the summary, the **By category** and **By work item** buttons choose the view, and the tab opens on By category. Two filters sit on the right. **Written by** limits the points to **All**, **Agents** or **People**. The **All points** and **To decide** toggle limits the points by decision. **To decide** hides every point that has an Action item or a Not needed reason. It also hides Went well points. The view and both filters are part of the page address, so a link to the tab opens the same view.

### By category

The view has four columns, one for each category. A column header gives the number of points left after the filters. When some still need a decision, it also shows how many are left to decide, and that count ignores the filters. Inside a column, ShipLean groups points under their theme, with points that have no theme last under **Unthemed**. A theme that keeps coming up carries a **Recurring** chip.

When the sprint has a Feature retro, a **Feature retro** box sits above the columns. It names the Feature, who compiled the retro, and how many story retros it came from. Inside a theme group, the Feature's points come first.

### By work item

The view shows one board for each item that has a retro in this sprint, with a Feature's board first. A Feature's board header carries a **Feature** chip. A sprint-level retro appears as a board marked **Sprint**. Each board has the same four columns, without theme groups. Its header shows the item key and title, who wrote the retro and when, and how many of its points have a decision. A **Rolled over** chip marks a retro that an agent logged before the work was finished. **Open** goes to the item's retro page.

Under the columns, a Feature board lists the story retros the conductor compiled it from. At the bottom of a board, **Raw failure log** is collapsed. Choose it to read the blockers, disproved assumptions and friction exactly as the agent logged them. A board whose retro has no failure log shows no such section.

### The rail

The rail on the right of both views has three sections.

- **Coverage** lists the stories, bugs and Action items finished in the sprint, and the Features that had a story finish or roll over from it. A tick means the item has a retro. **missing** means it has none. **waiting** means a Feature's last story is still open, and a Feature with an open story shows it even if it has a retro.
- **Action items** lists the Action items raised from this sprint's points. It also lists other open Action items whose theme is on one of this sprint's points. Each shows its key, its state, the sprint it is planned in or **Not planned**, and the sprint it came from.
- **Recurring** lists the themes mentioned in 3 of the last 4 sprints with no change that helped. A strip shows the mentions in each sprint, and a dashed line marks where a fix shipped.

### A sprint with no points

When nobody has logged anything, the four columns on By category are empty and each still ends with **+ Add**. The By work item view says **Nothing here.** and has no **+ Add**, so add the first point on By category. In the rail, Action items says **Nothing here.**, Recurring still lists themes that recur from earlier sprints, and Coverage lists the finished items. Recurring says **Nothing here.** when the project has no history, and Coverage says it when no item finished. **Finished items with a retro** reads 0 out of the number of items in Coverage that are not waiting. **Points to decide** and **Action items raised** read zero. Adding the first point creates your retro for the sprint, so there is nothing to set up first.

## Adding, theming and archiving a point

### Add a point

1. Open the **Retro** tab of the sprint.
2. Choose **+ Add** under the last point of the column that fits, such as **Went badly**. Each board has one editor open at a time.
3. Type the point in the box. The **Add** button stays off until the box has text.
4. For a Went badly point, fill in **Root cause** if you know it. The field is optional.
5. On the By category view, choose what the point is **About**. The default is **The sprint**, and the list also holds the items in Coverage and the items that have a retro in this sprint. On the By work item view, the point goes on the board you added it from.
6. Choose **Add**.

The note says **Goes on your retro**. ShipLean adds the point to your own retro for that sprint or item, and makes the retro if you have none. You cannot add a point to an agent's retro. By default a new point has no theme, so it appears under **Unthemed** on the By category view. An instance that enables automatic themes can theme the point at once. See [Retrospectives](/ui/retrospectives/). If your filters would hide the new point, the tab sets them back to **All** and **All points**.

### Move a point to another theme

On the By work item view, each note shows its theme under the text, written as `# Theme name` or `# Unthemed`, with a small arrow. The By category view shows no such line, because the group heading is the theme. To change a theme, switch to By work item.

1. Choose the theme line on the note.
2. Type to filter the list, or use the arrow keys to reach the theme you want. Each theme shows how many points it holds.
3. Choose the theme. To start a new one, type a name that matches none and choose **Add 'name'**.

The point moves at once, whoever wrote it. You can move an agent's point to another theme, but you cannot change its text.

### Archive a point

1. Choose the **⋯** button at the top right of a note.
2. Choose **Archive**.

An archived point leaves every view and count. Only points that people wrote have the **⋯** button, so you cannot archive an agent's point.

## Deciding points

Every point except a Went well point needs a decision. A point is either undecided, covered by an Action item, or marked **Not needed**. Went well points show no decision buttons.

An undecided point shows two choices at the bottom of its note.

- **+ Action item** raises an Action item from the point. See [Raising an Action item](#raising-an-action-item).
- **Not needed** records that the team will do nothing about the point.

To mark a point **Not needed**:

1. Choose **Not needed** on the note.
2. Type the reason in the box. **Save** stays off until the box has text.
3. Choose **Save**.

The note then shows **Not needed** with the reason, who decided it and when, and the point fades. To change your mind, choose **Undo** on the note, and the point is undecided again.

A point with an Action item shows a pin with the item's type icon, key and state. If the item covers other points, the pin adds `+2 more points` or similar. Choose the pin to open the Action item. You cannot decide a point that has a decision, so to move it from Not needed to an Action item, choose **Undo** first.

To see what is left, choose **To decide** on the toggle. Each column header also shows how many of its points are waiting.

## Raising an Action item

An Action item is the work you do about a retro point. It is a work item of the Action item type, at the same level as a Story or Bug. It has four states, **New**, **Active**, **Closed** and **Removed**, and a parent that is a Feature or none. Tasks can sit under it. Its points count toward the sprint's committed and completed figures, the burndown, velocity and the forecast. It appears on the Stories backlog, the Board and the Taskboard, and can go in a sprint. The Jira import never creates one.

One Action item can cover many points. A new one starts in **New** with no sprint, so pulling it into a sprint is the team's approval.

### Raise one from a point

The button is most prominent on a **Can be improved** point, where it is a filled button. It is a plain link on Went badly and Not in the plan points.

1. Choose **+ Action item** on the note.
2. In the **Action item** dialog, keep **New** selected at the top.
3. Edit the **Title**. It starts as the first 80 characters of the point, and it is required.
4. Choose a **Type of fix**. The choices are **Skill**, **Guide**, **Test**, **Analyzer**, **Product** and **Other**, each with a hint under it. The dialog starts with **Guide** chosen.
5. Check the **Theme**. The Action item has one theme, and the dialog starts with the most common theme among the points it covers. Pick another from the list, or pick **No theme**.
6. Under **Points it covers**, tick or clear points. The dialog ticks the point you started from and lists it first, then lists other undecided Can be improved, Went badly and Not in the plan points. On By category it offers points from the sprint. On By work item it offers points from that board's item and its stories and bugs. The count of selected points sits beside the heading.
7. Choose **Create action item**. The button stays off until the title is filled in and at least one point is ticked.

### How the point and the theme link

The Action item quotes each point it covers and links back to it. Every ticked point counts as decided and shows a pin to the new item, and the new item appears in the **Action items** section of the rail. The theme you chose becomes the Action item's theme. Insights compares how often that theme comes up before and after the Action item closes, and shows whether it helped.

### Add points to an Action item that exists

1. Choose **+ Action item** on the note.
2. Choose **Add to existing** at the top of the dialog.
3. Pick the open Action item from the **Action item** list. If none is open, the list says **No open action items**.
4. Tick the points to add.
5. Choose **Add points**.

A point belongs to at most one live Action item. If every point is decided already, the dialog says there is nothing to cover.

Removing an Action item returns its points to undecided, so they show **+ Action item** and **Not needed** again.
